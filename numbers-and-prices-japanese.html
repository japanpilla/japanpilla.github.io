Japanese Numbers, Prices and Time (with Lakh and Crore Explained)

By japanpilla, a Telugu creator based in Tokyo · Updated October 2026

Numbers are the first thing you need in a shop or station. They are easier than most beginners expect, because Japanese counting is regular. There is one surprise for Indian learners: Japanese groups large numbers in tens of thousands, not in lakhs and crores. This lesson explains how to convert between the two.

1. Numbers 1 to 10

Memorise these first. The numbers 11 to 99 are built from them: 11 is juu-ichi (10+1), 20 is ni-juu (2×10), and 35 is san-juu-go (3×10+5). No new words are needed.

2. Hundreds, thousands, man, lakh and crore

In English and India, large numbers are grouped differently, but Japanese has its own unit: man (万), which is 10,000. Once you know that, you can handle any price. For Indian readers this table links man to lakh and crore.

KanjiRomajiNumberతెలుగుHiragana / notes
百Hyaku100వంద / నూరు300 is sanbyaku, 600 is roppyaku, 800 is happyaku (sound changes).
千Sen1,000వెయ్యి3,000 is sanzen, 8,000 is hassen.
万Man10,000పది వేలుJapanese counts in units of 10,000, not 1,000.
十万Juuman100,000 (1 lakh)ఒక లక్ష10 man = 1 lakh.
百万Hyakuman1,000,000 (10 lakh)పది లక్షలు100 man = 1 million = 10 lakh.
千万Senman10,000,000 (1 crore)ఒక కోటి1,000 man = 1 crore.
億Oku100,000,000 (10 crore)పది కోట్లుUsed for company sales and large budgets.

Easy trick: 1 lakh = 10 man. 1 crore = 1,000 man. So a salary of ¥300,000 is san-juu-man (30 man), and a flat that costs ¥90,000 per month is kyuu-man (9 man) a month.

3. Saying prices

The currency word is 円 (en, yen). Say the number and add en.

  • 150円 = hyaku gojuu en
  • 480円 = yonhyaku hachijuu en
  • 1,200円 = sen nihyaku en
  • 5,000円 = gosen en
  • 12,000円 = ichiman nisen en

To ask the price: Kore wa ikura desu ka? (దీని ధర ఎంత?). To say that something is expensive or cheap: takai desu (ఖరీదైనది) and yasui desu (చౌక).

Youすみません、これはいくらですか？Sumimasen, kore wa ikura desu ka?Excuse me, how much is this?
Staff千二百円です。Sen nihyaku en desu.It's 1,200 yen.
Youもう少し安いのはありますか？Mou sukoshi yasui no wa arimasu ka?Do you have a slightly cheaper one?
Staffこちらは八百円です。Kochira wa happyaku en desu.This one is 800 yen.
Youじゃあ、それをください。Jaa, sore o kudasai.Then I'll take that one, please.

For live exchange rates, check a currency converter before you travel, since rates change daily.

4. Counting things and people

Japanese uses counters, small endings that depend on what you count, such as flat objects or long objects. Beginners can survive with the general set of -tsu numbers for objects, and -nin for people.

ひとつHitotsu1 thingఒకటిUsed for objects when you do not know the counter.
ふたつFutatsu2 thingsరెండు
みっつMittsu3 thingsమూడు
よっつYottsu4 thingsనాలుగు
いつつItsutsu5 thingsఐదు
ひとりHitori1 personఒక్కరుThe only person form with a special name, together with ふたり (futari, 2).
さんにんSan-nin3 peopleముగ్గురుFrom three on, say number + 人 (nin): 四人 is yo-nin.

5. Telling the time

Hours use 時 (ji), and minutes use 分 (fun/pun). Three hours have unusual forms: 4 o'clock is yoji, 7 is shichiji, 9 is kuji.

JapaneseRomajiMeaningతెలుగు అర్థంWhen to use it
一時Ichiji1 o'clockఒంటి గంటAdd 時 (ji) to a number.
四時Yoji4 o'clockనాలుగు గంటలుNot yonji or shiji.
七時Shichiji7 o'clockఏడు గంటలుNot nanaji in normal speech.
九時Kuji9 o'clockతొమ్మిది గంటలుNot kyuuji.
半HanHalf pastసగం (అర గంట)三時半 (sanji han) = 3:30.
何時ですかNanji desu ka?What time is it?ఇప్పుడు సమయం ఎంత?Add 今 (ima) for 'now'.

Remember that some Japanese timetables use a 24-hour style, and late-night times can even appear as 24:10 (just after midnight). There is no daylight saving time in Japan, and Japan Standard Time is 3 hours and 30 minutes ahead of India.
