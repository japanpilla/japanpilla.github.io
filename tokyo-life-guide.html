Life in Tokyo: Culture Shocks and Practical Lessons

Moving from South India to the heart of Tokyo brings a unique set of adventures. While Tokyo is renowned worldwide for bullet trains, neon signs, and cleanliness, daily reality has distinct quirks that every newcomer should know.

1. The Art of Quiet Commuting

In Tokyo's metro network (Tokyo Metro and JR Yamanote Line), silence is customary. Phone calls inside train coaches are strictly avoided, and conversations happen in hushed whispers. Knowing this basic cultural rule saves newcomers from awkward glances.

2. Finding Spices and Indian Ingredients

One common question from Telugu speakers moving to Japan is finding familiar grocery items. In neighborhoods like Nishi-Kasai and Shin-Okubo, Indian grocers stock everything from sona masoori rice and toor dal to curry leaves and mango pickles.

3. The Complex Trash Sorting System

Japan sorts household waste into Burnable (combustible), Non-burnable, Plastics, PET bottles, and Aluminium cans. Each municipal ward provides a dedicated calendar specifying which bag goes out on which weekday morning.
