Japanese for Trains and Stations: Directions, Signs and Useful Sentences

By japanpilla, a Telugu creator based in Tokyo · Updated October 2026

Trains are the easiest way to travel in Tokyo and the biggest source of beginner stress. The system is excellent, but large stations can feel like cities underground. This lesson covers the sentences, signs and habits that help you travel confidently, with Telugu explanations.

On this page
  • How trains work (and the IC card)
  • Asking for help
  • Signs to recognise

  • Train etiquette

1. How trains work, and the IC card

Tokyo is served by several operators, including JR East, Tokyo Metro and Toei Subway. Each has its own lines but you can switch between them. The easiest way to pay is a rechargeable IC card such as Suica or PASMO. You tap it at the gate when you enter and again when you leave, and the fare is deducted automatically. Many phones can also store an IC card in their wallet. The same cards can pay at konbini, vending machines and on buses.

Every station has a name in Japanese, romaji and a number-and-letter code, for example JY01 on the Yamanote Line. If you cannot read kanji yet, follow the code on the signs and in navigation apps.

2. Asking for help

Station staff are used to questions, and so are other passengers. Start with sumimasen (excuse me), say where you want to go, and keep the sentence short.

次の駅はどこですかTsugi no eki wa doko desu ka?What is the next station?తర్వాతి స్టేషన్ ఏది?Handy if announcements are only in Japanese.

3. Signs to recognise

Learning even eight kanji will make stations much easier. Most signs also have English, but these ones show up everywhere.

4. A conversation at the station

Youすみません、この電車は新宿に行きますか？Sumimasen, kono densha wa Shinjuku ni ikimasu ka?Excuse me, does this train go to Shinjuku?
Staffいいえ、反対側のホームです。Iie, hantaigawa no hoomu desu.No, it's the platform on the opposite side.
You何番線ですか？Nan-ban-sen desu ka?Which platform number?
Staff二番線です。階段を上がってください。Ni-ban-sen desu. Kaidan o agatte kudasai.Platform 2. Please go up the stairs.
Youありがとうございます。Arigatou gozaimasu.Thank you.

New words here: hantaigawa (opposite side), hoomu (platform, from "platform home"), kaidan (stairs), agatte kudasai (please go up). You do not have to memorise them now, just follow the sentence shape.

5. Train etiquette

  • Keep your voice low. Loud calls on trains are widely avoided. Put your phone on silent, which is called manner mode.
  • Queue at the marked spots. Let passengers off first, then board in line.
  • Take your backpack off in crowded carriages and hold it in front or put it on the rack.
  • Priority seats are for people who need them. Older people, pregnant passengers, and those with injuries or disabilities.
  • Rush hour is real. On busy lines between roughly 7:30 and 9:30 in the morning, trains are very crowded. Avoid it if you can.

Tip: before you travel, save the station name in Japanese on your phone. If you get lost, show it to staff and say Koko ni ikitai desu (నేను ఇక్కడికి వెళ్లాలనుకుంటున్నాను), "I want to go here".
