Japanese for Ordering Food: Restaurants, Ramen Shops and Vegetarian Requests

By japanpilla, a Telugu creator based in Tokyo · Updated October 2026

Eating out is one of the best parts of Japan. It is also where beginners feel most nervous. This lesson gives you the sentences for walking in, ordering, explaining what you cannot eat, and paying, with Telugu explanations and a few habits that make dining easier.

1. Walking in and ordering

Staff will shout irasshaimase as you enter. You do not need to reply. Some shops are sit-down restaurants, and some, especially ramen and gyudon shops, use a ticket machine (食券機, shokkenki) at the entrance. You pick the dish and pay at the machine, then give the ticket to staff. If you are not sure, ask sumimasen, kore wa dou shimasu ka? ("Excuse me, what do I do here?").

JapaneseRomajiMeaningతెలుగు అర్థంWhen to use it
何名様ですかNanmei-sama desu ka?How many people?ఎంతమంది వచ్చారు?Staff ask this. Answer with a number below.
一人ですHitori desuJust one personఒక్కరే.ひとり (hitori) = one person.
二人ですFutari desuTwo peopleఇద్దరం.三人です (san-nin desu) = three. After that, say the number plus 人 (nin).
メニューをくださいMenyuu o kudasaiA menu, pleaseదయచేసి మెనూ ఇవ్వండి.Many restaurants have picture menus, and some have English or QR codes.
おすすめは何ですかOsusume wa nan desu ka?What do you recommend?మీరు ఏది సిఫార్సు చేస్తారు?Good for a first visit to a ramen or set-meal shop.
これをくださいKore o kudasaiThis one, pleaseఇది ఇవ్వండి.Point to the item on the menu and say it.
水をくださいMizu o kudasaiWater, pleaseదయచేసి నీళ్లు ఇవ్వండి.Many restaurants bring free water or tea without being asked.
大盛りでお願いしますOomori de onegaishimasuA large portion, pleaseపెద్ద పరిమాణం ఇవ్వండి.Some shops charge a little extra, others are free.

2. Vegetarian, egg-free and spice requests

Japan has fewer vegetarian options than India, and the language can be a barrier. Learn the words for the foods you avoid and say them clearly. Showing a short note on your phone also works well.

JapaneseRomajiMeaningతెలుగు అర్థంWhen to use it
私はベジタリアンですWatashi wa bejitarian desuI am vegetarianనేను శాకాహారిని.Say it first. Staff may still not understand fish or stock, so be specific.
肉は食べませんNiku wa tabemasenI don't eat meatనేను మాంసం తినను.肉 (niku) is meat. Add 豚 (buta, pork) or 牛 (gyuu, beef) for detail.
魚は食べませんSakana wa tabemasenI don't eat fishనేను చేపలు తినను.魚 (sakana) is fish.
卵は食べませんTamago wa tabemasenI don't eat eggsనేను గుడ్లు తినను.卵 (tamago) is egg.
魚のだしも使っていませんかSakana no dashi mo tsukatte imasen ka?Is fish stock not used either?చేపల రసం (డాషి) కూడా వాడలేదా?The most important question for vegetarians. See the tip below.
辛くしてくださいKaraku shite kudasaiPlease make it spicyకారంగా చేయండి.Japanese spice is mild, so asking helps. Few shops will match Indian heat.
辛くしないでくださいKaraku shinaide kudasaiPlease don't make it spicyకారం వేయకండి.For children or anyone sensitive to heat.
アレルギーがありますArerugii ga arimasuI have an allergyనాకు అలెర్జీ ఉంది.Add what you are allergic to, like ピーナッツ (peanuts).

3. The dashi problem for vegetarians

Many dishes that look vegetarian, like miso soup, udon broth, simmered vegetables and even some rice dishes, are made with dashi, a stock that often contains dried bonito fish. The staff may say "no meat" and still be using it. This is why the sentence Sakana no dashi mo tsukatte imasen ka? is so useful. Even then, shops may not be able to guarantee anything, so check before you order, and consider choosing dishes like plain rice, vegetable tempura, salads or specialist vegetarian restaurants.

Cooking at home is easier because you control the ingredients. Look for the ingredient list on packages and search for the characters 魚 (fish), 肉 (meat) and 卵 (egg), and for だし (dashi) and エキス (extract).

4. A full ramen shop conversation

Staffいらっしゃいませ！何名様ですか？Irasshaimase! Nanmei-sama desu ka?Welcome! How many people?
You二人です。Futari desu.Two people.
Staffこちらへどうぞ。Kochira e douzo.This way, please.
Youすみません、メニューをください。Sumimasen, menyuu o kudasai.Excuse me, a menu please.
You私は魚を食べません。これは大丈夫ですか？Watashi wa sakana o tabemasen. Kore wa daijoubu desu ka?I don't eat fish. Is this one okay?
Staffはい、大丈夫です。Hai, daijoubu desu.Yes, it's fine.
Youじゃあ、これを二つください。Jaa, kore o futatsu kudasai.Then two of these, please.

Futatsu means "two (things)". The full counting system is in our numbers and prices lesson.

5. Eating well and paying

いただきますItadakimasu(said before eating)తినడానికి ముందు చెప్పే కృతజ్ఞత మాటNot a translation of 'bon appetit'. It thanks everyone who made the meal.
おいしいですOishii desuIt's deliciousచాలా రుచిగా ఉంది.The best compliment to the cook. おいしかったです = 'It was delicious'.

6. Manners worth knowing

  • There is no tipping. Do not leave money on the table. It can confuse staff, who may run after you to return it.
  • Slurping noodles is normal. It is even seen as a sign that you are enjoying the ramen.
  • Do not stick chopsticks upright in rice. It resembles a funeral offering. Rest them on the holder.
  • Pay at the register in most casual restaurants, not at the table.
  • Use the hot towel (oshibori) to clean your hands only, not your face.
