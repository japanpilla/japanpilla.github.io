Everyday Japanese for Convenience Stores and Daily Life (in Telugu)

By japanpilla, a Telugu creator based in Tokyo · Updated October 2026

Convenience stores, called konbini, are where most beginners practise real Japanese. They are open all day, everything is on the shelf, and the conversation is short and predictable. This lesson covers what you will say there, plus the polite words that work everywhere in Japan.

1. At the konbini

You rarely need long sentences. The pattern is: point at what you want, say kore o kudasai, then answer two or three simple questions from the cashier. Most shops also have self-checkout and card readers, so you can even practise without speaking much.

JapaneseRomajiMeaningతెలుగు అర్థంWhen to use it
いらっしゃいませIrasshaimaseWelcome! (staff greeting)స్వాగతం!Staff say it when you enter. You do not need to reply, a small nod is enough.
これをくださいKore o kudasaiThis one, pleaseఇది ఇవ్వండి.Point and say it. Works in every shop. これ = this, ください = please give.
これはいくらですかKore wa ikura desu ka?How much is this?దీని ధర ఎంత?いくら means 'how much'. Prices are usually written on the shelf.
袋はいりませんFukuro wa irimasenI don't need a bagనాకు సంచి అవసరం లేదు.Shops charge a few yen for a plastic bag, so many people carry their own.
温めてくださいAtatamete kudasaiPlease heat this upదయచేసి ఇది వేడి చేయండి.For bento, curry and rice balls. Staff use the microwave.
お箸をくださいOhashi o kudasaiChopsticks, pleaseదయచేసి చాప్‌స్టిక్స్ ఇవ్వండి.They are not always put in the bag automatically.
スプーンをくださいSupuun o kudasaiA spoon, pleaseదయచేసి చెంచా ఇవ్వండి.Handy for rice and curry if you do not like chopsticks.
カードでお願いしますKaado de onegaishimasuI'll pay by card, pleaseకార్డుతో చెల్లిస్తాను, దయచేసి.Say it before they ring up the total. 現金で (genkin de) means 'with cash'.
レシートをくださいReshiito o kudasaiA receipt, pleaseదయచేసి రసీదు ఇవ్వండి.Receipts are not always given automatically.

2. A full konbini conversation

This is a typical exchange. Try reading it aloud, then cover the English and test yourself.

Cashierいらっしゃいませ。温めますか？Irasshaimase. Atatamemasu ka?Welcome. Shall I heat it up?
Youはい、お願いします。Hai, onegaishimasu.Yes, please.
Cashierお箸はご利用ですか？Ohashi wa go-riyou desu ka?Do you need chopsticks?
Youスプーンをください。Supuun o kudasai.A spoon, please.
Cashier480円です。Yonhyaku hachijuu en desu.That is 480 yen.
Youカードでお願いします。Kaado de onegaishimasu.By card, please.
Cashierありがとうございました。Arigatou gozaimashita.Thank you very much.

Notice that the cashier ends with the past tense gozaimashita. Staff say it when the transaction is finished. You can answer with a small nod or doumo.

3. Polite words that work everywhere

These few words will do more for you than any grammar rule. Japanese has a strong habit of saying thank you and sorry often, so do not worry about using them too much.

JapaneseRomajiMeaningతెలుగు అర్థంWhen to use it
すみませんSumimasenExcuse me / sorryక్షమించండి / ఒక్కసారి ఇటు చూడండిThe most useful word. Call a waiter, pass someone in a crowd, or apologise lightly. It can also mean a polite 'thank you' for someone's trouble.
ありがとうございますArigatou gozaimasuThank you (polite)చాలా ధన్యవాదాలు.Use it with shop staff and strangers. With friends, ありがとう is enough.
どうもDoumoThanks (casual)థాంక్స్A quick, light thanks when someone hands you something.
お願いしますOnegaishimasuPlease (when asking)దయచేసిAdd it after a request: これ、お願いします (Kore, onegaishimasu).
ごめんなさいGomennasaiI'm sorryనన్ను క్షమించండి.A true apology. Sumimasen is lighter, gomennasai is more personal.
大丈夫ですDaijoubu desuI'm fine / no thank youపర్వాలేదు / నాకు వద్దుIt has two meanings. If staff offer a bag and you say 大丈夫です, it means 'no thanks'.
はい / いいえHai / IieYes / Noఅవును / కాదుはい often means 'I hear you', not always 'I agree'. Listen for the rest of the sentence.

4. When you do not understand

You will not understand everything at first, and that is fine. Learning how to ask for help is a skill in itself. Keep these sentences ready, and use the kudasai or onegaishimasu ending so you sound polite.

JapaneseRomajiMeaningతెలుగు అర్థంWhen to use it
わかりませんWakarimasenI don't understandనాకు అర్థం కాలేదు.Say it calmly. People will usually slow down or find an easier word.
もう一度お願いしますMou ichido onegaishimasuOne more time, pleaseదయచేసి మళ్లీ చెప్పండి.Ask for a repeat instead of guessing.
ゆっくりお願いしますYukkuri onegaishimasuSlowly, pleaseదయచేసి నెమ్మదిగా చెప్పండి.Native speakers talk fast, and this one phrase helps a lot.
英語は話せますかEigo wa hanasemasu ka?Do you speak English?మీరు ఇంగ్లీష్ మాట్లాడగలరా?Younger staff in Tokyo often know basic English.
日本語は少しだけ話せますNihongo wa sukoshi dake hanasemasuI speak only a little Japaneseనాకు జపనీస్ కొంచెం మాత్రమే వచ్చు.Said with a smile, this often makes people more helpful.
これは日本語で何と言いますかKore wa nihongo de nan to iimasu ka?What is this called in Japanese?దీన్ని జపనీస్‌లో ఏమంటారు?The best sentence for learning new words. Point at things.
ちょっと待ってくださいChotto matte kudasaiPlease wait a momentకొంచెం ఆగండి.When you need a second to find a card or an address.
助けてくださいTasukete kudasaiPlease help meనాకు సహాయం చేయండి.For real trouble. The emergency numbers are 110 (police) and 119 (ambulance and fire).

5. Pronunciation tips for Telugu speakers

  • Vowels are almost the same. Japanese has five pure vowels, a, i, u, e, o, similar to the short Telugu vowels అ ఇ ఉ ఎ ఒ. You can say them confidently.
  • Long and short vowels change the word. おばさん (obasan, aunt) and おばあさん (obaasan, grandmother) differ only in length. Telugu has the same short and long difference, so you are ahead of English speakers here.
  • Double consonants need a short pause. きて (kite, come) and きって (kitte, stamp). Think of Telugu words like అక్క and అమ్మ.
  • No aspirated sounds. Japanese has no sounds like ఖ or ఘ, so say ka and ga plainly.
  • The r is soft. Japanese r is a quick tap of the tongue, close to a soft Telugu ర.

Practice idea: next time you watch an anime scene in a shop, pause and repeat the shopkeeper's lines. Then compare them with the phrases in this lesson.
