By japanpilla, a Telugu creator based in Tokyo · Updated October 2026

Anime India is coming to Hyderabad on 14 and 15 November 2026. Whether this is your first convention or your tenth, this page gives you the basics, a few planning tips for first-timers, and Japanese phrases for meeting guests and fellow fans. What is on at the convention?

According to the event listing, the convention has full-day programming over three stages, plus an expo floor. Highlights named by the organisers include:

  • A guest from Japan. The listing mentions an invited VIP guest.
  • Live concert. A music performance is part of the line-up.
  • Cosplay zone. A dedicated space for cosplayers and photos.
  • Maid and butler café. A café experience inspired by Japanese themed cafés.
  • Panels and fan content. Industry and fan panels, along with gaming, fan art and official merchandise.

Schedules and guest names can change, so check the organiser's announcements for the final timetable.

First-timer tips

  1. Buy your ticket early. Conventions can sell out or raise prices closer to the date. Keep a screenshot of the ticket on your phone, in case the signal is weak at the venue.
  2. Plan your two days. Choose two or three must-see events per day, such as a panel or a concert, and leave the rest of the time for the expo floor and food.
  3. Arrive early on day one. The event starts at 10 AM, and queues for entry, popular merch and photo spots tend to build quickly.
  4. Wear comfortable shoes. You will walk and stand a lot. If you are in a costume, carry a spare pair.
  5. Carry water, a small snack and a power bank. Photos and tickets drain a phone fast.
  6. Bring some cash as well as UPI or a card. Small stalls sometimes have network problems.
  7. Check the rules for props and costumes. Venues usually restrict real-looking weapons and large items. Read the organiser's FAQ before you go.
  8. Plan your route. Hyderabad traffic can be heavy, so allow extra travel time and check the route on a map the night before.

Cosplay etiquette

  • Always ask before taking a photo. A simple "Can I take your photo?" is enough.
  • Do not touch costumes, wigs or props without permission.
  • Cosplay is not consent. Nobody should be touched, followed or filmed without a yes.
  • Give compliments. Telling someone you love their costume makes their day.
  • Be patient in queues and respect the staff and volunteers.

Meeting a Japanese guest: a small bow is a respectful greeting, and a handshake is fine if they offer. Use both hands when you hand over something like an autograph item, and keep your voice friendly and calm. Do not touch the guest, and let staff manage the queue.

Why this event matters

Anime conventions give Indian fans a place to meet in person, share fan art and cosplay, and connect with Japanese culture directly. Many of us grew up watching anime on TV and online, and an event like this makes the community visible. If you go, say hello to people, and consider learning a few Japanese phrases to use there.
