30+ Japanese Anime Phrases Explained in Telugu

By japanpilla, a Telugu creator based in Tokyo · Updated October 2026

If you watch anime with subtitles, you already hear Japanese every day. This lesson explains the most common phrases, what they really mean, and which ones you can safely use in real life. Each entry has a Telugu meaning and a note on when to use it.

1. Reactions and battle words

These are the words characters shout when something surprising or dramatic happens. Most are casual, so use them with friends, not with your boss.

JapaneseRomajiMeaningతెలుగు అర్థంWhen and how it is used
すごいSugoiWow / amazing / incredibleఅద్భుతం! చాలా గొప్పగా ఉంది!Used for anything impressive: a strong attack, a clever plan, a great cosplay. To a senior, say すごいですね (Sugoi desu ne).
まさかMasakaNo way! / It can't be!ఇది నిజమా? నమ్మలేకపోతున్నా!Said at a plot twist, when something you thought impossible happens.
うそUsoSeriously?! (literally 'a lie')నిజంగానా? అబద్ధం కదా!Everyday surprise too: 友達が結婚した → うそ！ (Your friend got married → No way!). It is not an accusation.
ありえないArienaiImpossible / unbelievableఇది అసాధ్యం! అస్సలు జరగదు!Works for good or bad surprises. Common among young people in Tokyo.
やったYattaI did it! / Yay!సాధించేశాను! హుర్రే!Shout it when you win, pass an exam, or get a ticket you wanted.
負けないMakenaiI won't lose!నేను ఓడిపోను!The negative form of 負ける (makeru, to lose). A classic hero line.
あきらめないAkiramenaiI won't give upనేను వదిలిపెట్టను, వెనక్కి తగ్గను.From あきらめる (to give up). Fits exams and job hunting too.
強いTsuyoiStrong / powerfulచాలా బలంగా ఉంది.An adjective with no gender. It describes a person, an attack, even strong coffee.
危ないAbunaiCareful! / Dangerous!జాగ్రత్త! ప్రమాదం!Genuinely useful in real life, for example if a bicycle is coming toward you.
待ってMatteWait!ఆగు! ఆగండి!Casual. For strangers or seniors use 待ってください (Matte kudasai).
やめてYameteStop itఆపు, వద్దు!In anime you also hear the rough やめろ (yamero). In real life use やめてください (Yamete kudasai).
行くぞIku zoHere I go! / Let's go!పదండి! నేను వెళ్తున్నా!The ending ぞ sounds strong and masculine. Women and polite speakers usually avoid it.

2. Feelings, friends and family

Anime is full of relationships, so these words appear in nearly every series. Notice that Japanese has special words for older and younger people, much like Telugu has అన్నయ్య, అక్క and తమ్ముడు.

JapaneseRomajiMeaningతెలుగు అర్థంWhen and how it is used
好きSukiLike / loveఇష్టం好きです means 'I like it'. It is also what people say when confessing feelings, so mind the context.
大好きDaisukiReally loveనాకు చాలా ఇష్టం.Fine for food, anime, hobbies. 大好きなアニメ = 'my favourite anime'.
かわいいKawaiiCuteముద్దుగా ఉంది!Used for babies, pets, outfits, even stationery.
かっこいいKakkoiiCool / stylishచాలా స్టైల్‌గా ఉంది! సూపర్!A compliment for people, cars, a hero's pose.
尊いToutoi'So precious' (fan slang)ఎంతో అపురూపం!Fans use it for a moment between two characters that is almost too sweet.
仲間NakamaComrade / close friendనమ్మకమైన సహచరులు, మన టీమ్.A team bound by trust. Common in shounen stories.
友達TomodachiFriendస్నేహితుడు / స్నేహితురాలుThe ordinary word. 友達になりましょう = 'Let's be friends'.
お兄ちゃんOniichanBig brotherఅన్నయ్యA child's word. Older brothers are called this in the family.
お姉ちゃんOneechanBig sisterఅక్కయ్యThe sister version. Also said to affectionate older girls.
先輩SenpaiSenior (at school or work)నాకంటే ముందు చేరిన సీనియర్The relation matters in Japan: senpai guide, kouhai respect them.
後輩KouhaiJuniorమన తర్వాత చేరిన జూనియర్Rarely used to address someone. You use it to describe them.
先生SenseiTeacher / doctor / expertగురువు, టీచర్, డాక్టర్A title, not just 'teacher'. Doctors and manga artists are 先生 too.

3. Fan culture words

These are the words you hear at conventions, on social media and in fan groups. If you are going to a convention, this is the vocabulary that will help you make friends.

JapaneseRomajiMeaningతెలుగు అర్థంWhen and how it is used
推しOshiYour favourite character or idolమీకు అత్యంత ఇష్టమైన పాత్ర లేదా కళాకారుడు'Who is your oshi?' 推しは誰？ (Oshi wa dare?). 推し活 is the whole hobby of supporting them.
神回Kamikai'God episode'అద్భుతమైన ఎపిసోడ్Said of an episode so good fans talk about it for weeks.
最終回SaishuukaiFinal episodeచివరి ఎపిసోడ్最終回が泣けた = 'The final episode made me cry'.
原作GensakuThe original work (manga/novel)అసలు కథ (మంగా లేదా నవల)Fans debate 'anime vs 原作'.
声優SeiyuuVoice actorవాయిస్ యాక్టర్Seiyuu are celebrities in Japan with fan events and concerts.
ハマるHamaruTo get hookedపూర్తిగా లీనమైపోవడం, వదలలేకపోవడంこのアニメにハマった = 'I got hooked on this anime'.
聖地巡礼Seichi junreiAnime pilgrimageఅనిమేలో చూపిన నిజమైన ప్రదేశాలను సందర్శించడంFans travel to real places that appeared in a series. Many Tokyo spots are visited this way.
コスプレKosupureCosplayఇష్టమైన పాత్ర వేషధారణA short form of 'costume play'. Always ask before photographing someone.

4. Honorifics: -san, -kun, -chan and more

Japanese adds a small ending to names to show respect or closeness. Telugu speakers already know this idea: we add గారు to a name to be polite, for example రావు గారు. The Japanese system works the same way, just with more choices.

EndingRomajiMeaningతెలుగు పోలికHow to use it
〜さん-sanPolite default, for anyoneగారు లాంటిది (మర్యాదగా)Use it with everyone you do not know well. Safe, always.
〜くん-kunCasual, for boys and younger menతమ్ముడు వంటి చనువుSaid by seniors to juniors, or among friends.
〜ちゃん-chanCute, affectionateముద్దుగా పిలిచే రూపంFor children, close friends, pets. Do not use with strangers.
〜様-samaVery respectfulఅత్యంత గౌరవంCustomers, gods, royalty. Staff say お客様 (okyakusama) to customers.
〜先生-senseiTeacher / expertగురువు గారుTeachers, doctors, artists. Do not add -san after it.

Rule of thumb: when in doubt, add -san. Never call yourself -san, and never use -chan with someone you have just met.

5. Anime Japanese vs real Japanese

Anime characters speak in a stylised way so that you can tell them apart. Real people in Tokyo do not talk like that. A few differences to keep in mind:

  • "I" has many forms. 私 (watashi) is neutral and safe. 僕 (boku) is used by boys and men. 俺 (ore) is rough and male. Many heroes say 俺 and sound tough, but it can sound rude in a formal setting.
  • "You" is rarely said. Anime characters use お前 (omae) or 貴様 (kisama), which are rude. In real life you use the person's name plus -san instead of "you".
  • Endings like ぜ, ぞ, だってばよ are character traits. Real speakers do not use them. A woman saying 行くぞ would sound unusual.
  • Use です and ます for politeness. Turning a friendly sentence into a polite one is easy: 行く (iku) becomes 行きます (ikimasu), and 好き becomes 好きです.

The safest way to practise is to learn a phrase from anime, then check how people say it politely in our everyday Japanese lesson.
